**Mantis** is a pair of handheld rigs for recording demonstrations without moving the robot. You hold one rig in each hand and do the task yourself. Axol's software tracks both rigs and records the episode in the same format as an episode recorded on Axol. A policy trained on Mantis data can run on Axol.

Each rig has:

- The same **gripper** that Axol uses.
- An analog **trigger** that opens and closes the gripper. The further you squeeze, the further the gripper closes.
- A mount for a **pose tracker**: a Meta Quest controller, a VIVE Tracker 3.0 or a VIVE Ultimate Tracker.

In the control panel, Mantis appears as its own device next to Axol, with its own tile and settings. Mantis supports **Teleoperation** and **Data collection**. The other operations are for Axol only.

## Overview

A Mantis kit has:

- **2× Mantis rigs**, one left and one right.
- **1× Mantis Hub**, which connects the rigs to the computer. **L** and **R** on its case mark the left and right rig cables. A USB cable connects the hub to the computer, such as a ZED Box.
- **A pose source**: one of the three tracking systems in [Mantis Tracking](/mantis/tracking). These are Quest controllers, VIVE Tracker 3.0 with 1 to 4 SteamVR base stations, or VIVE Ultimate Trackers with their wireless dongle.
- **Cameras**: up to two wrist ZED cameras and one head stereo camera. Data collection needs at least one camera.

Mantis has no arms. In a recording, the arm joint positions are computed from the rig poses, as if Axol had made the motion. The gripper positions are real and come from the grippers' motors.

The trigger reads `0.0` when fully released (gripper open) and `1.0` when fully squeezed (gripper closed). Each rig learns its trigger's released position when it powers on, so leave the triggers released while you power on. If a trigger doesn't reach `1.0` when you squeeze it fully, see [Testing the triggers and grippers](#testing-the-triggers-and-grippers).

### Tracker mounts

The kit includes the mount for the pose source you bought: Quest, Lighthouse (VIVE Tracker 3.0) or Ultimate. If you bought Mantis without a tracker, you can [print your own mount](#printing-your-own-mount).

| Pose source | Mount | Tracker-to-gripper offset |
|---|---|---|
| **Meta Quest** controllers | The controller is fixed to the rig. | Built in for Quest 3 (Touch Plus) controllers. Other controllers must be measured (see [Mantis Tracking](/mantis/tracking#transforms)). |
| **VIVE Tracker 3.0** (Lighthouse) | Flat-back mount in place of the controller. | Built in. Nothing to measure for the standard mount. |
| **VIVE Ultimate Tracker** | Same flat-back mount and orientation as Tracker 3.0. | Built in. Nothing to measure for the standard mount. |

#### Fastening a VIVE tracker to its mount

Both VIVE trackers attach to the flat-back mount the same way. You need:

- **1× 1/4"-20 × 1/2" screw**. It goes up through the mount into the tracker's standard 1/4"-20 socket.
- **1× mount pin** ([`mantis_mount_pin.stl`](https://downloads.almond.bot/resources/mantis_mount_pin.stl)), a 3D-printed pin 5 mm × 10 mm. It fits in the small hole next to the socket and stops the tracker from turning on the screw. If the tracker turns, the recorded gripper poses are wrong.

Press the pin into the mount first, seat the tracker on it, then tighten the screw.

#### Printing your own mount

If you ordered Mantis without a tracker and use your own, 3D-print the mount for it. Download the model for your pose source:

- Quest 3 controller mount, [left](https://downloads.almond.bot/resources/mantis_quest_3_mount_l.stl) and [right](https://downloads.almond.bot/resources/mantis_quest_3_mount_r.stl) (STL). The Touch controllers are mirror images, so print one of each.
- [VIVE Tracker 3.0 mount](https://downloads.almond.bot/resources/mantis_vive_3_mount.step) (STEP)
- [VIVE Ultimate Tracker mount](https://downloads.almond.bot/resources/mantis_vive_ultimate_mount.step) (STEP)

Each mount attaches to the rig with **M3×8 screws** ([McMaster-Carr 97763A813](https://www.mcmaster.com/97763A813/)). For a VIVE tracker, also print the mount pin, and attach the tracker as in [Fastening a VIVE tracker to its mount](#fastening-a-vive-tracker-to-its-mount).

## Installation

Mantis works with or without an Axol. Install the Axol software on the computer first (see [Installation](/installation)).

### Using Mantis without a robot

The rigs, the hub and a pose source are a complete data-collection setup. Besides the kit, you need a computer running Linux with systemd. Data collection needs at least one ZED camera, and the ZED cameras need GMSL inputs, so use an NVIDIA Jetson with GMSL inputs, such as a ZED Box. The grippers, triggers and tracking work on any Linux computer.

When Mantis is the only hardware connected, the [control panel](/guides/control-panel) finds it and connects on its own. With **Mantis** selected, the panel shows only the two operations Mantis supports:

- **Teleoperation**: the [trigger check](#testing-the-triggers-and-grippers). It drives the grippers from the triggers, without tracking.
- **[Data collection](/operations/data-collection)**.

Set up Mantis in this order:

1. [Install](/installation) the Axol software on the computer.
2. [Connect the hub](#connecting-the-hub).
3. [Select and connect Mantis](#selecting-and-connecting-mantis), and check the left and right sides.
4. [Test the triggers and grippers](#testing-the-triggers-and-grippers).
5. [Mount and assign the cameras](#mounting-the-cameras).
6. Set up the pose source in [Mantis Tracking](/mantis/tracking), then [collect data](/operations/data-collection).

### Connecting the hub

<Steps>
  <Step title="Cable the rigs to the hub">
    A cable comes out of the hub for each rig. Plug the **left** rig into the cable marked **L** and the **right** rig into the cable marked **R**. You can swap the sides in software later, but this wiring matches the default settings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect the hub to the computer">
    Plug the hub's USB cable into the computer, then power both rigs. The computer finds the hub and works out which rig is left and which is right. If you unplug the hub and plug it back in, it reconnects on its own.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let the control panel identify it">
    Open the [control panel](/guides/control-panel#connections) and connect to the **Axol Host**. The panel identifies a new hub on its own while both rigs are powered. If it can't, power the rigs: the panel checks again on its own and connects them. You can also run [`axol can.setup`](/cli/can-setup) in a terminal.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  `axol can.setup` resets every connected adapter, including Axol's arms, Jelly Mobile and the Mantis hub. Run it only when nothing is moving.
</Note>

### Selecting and connecting Mantis

<Steps>
  <Step title="Select Mantis">
    Press **Select** on the **Mantis** tile. Teleoperation and data collection now run on the rigs, and the Axol-only operations are hidden until you select Axol again. The choice is saved on the computer, so everyone who uses the control panel sees it.

    <Frame>
      <img src="/assets/mantis_tile.png" alt="The Mantis tile in the control panel's connections bar, selected and awaiting hardware" width="320" style={{ margin: "0 auto" }} />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect">
    When Mantis is selected and the hub is found, the panel connects on its own. If it doesn't, press **Connect** on the tile. The tile then shows the status of both grippers. A red square means a gripper can't be reached or has a fault. Fix it before you run an operation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the left and right sides">
    Click the title of the Mantis tile to open **Mantis settings**, then open **CAN**. The left and right fields must be filled in and different. The defaults are `can_mantis_l` and `can_mantis_r`. If squeezing the **left** trigger moves the **right** input, swap the two values, save and reconnect. You don't need to move any cables.

  </Step>
</Steps>

### Testing the triggers and grippers

Before you set up cameras or tracking, check that each trigger drives its own gripper. Mantis teleoperation is this check. It needs only the hub, with no tracking, cameras or headset.

1. With Mantis selected, choose **Teleoperation** and press **Start**. From a terminal, run:

   ```bash
   axol teleop --mantis
   ```

2. Keep hands and objects clear of the grippers, and release both triggers. Each gripper opens fully to find its end stop.
3. Squeeze each trigger. Its gripper should close as far as you squeeze.
4. Press **Stop**, or **Ctrl-C** in the terminal, to turn both grippers off.

If the triggers stop sending readings, the run stops on its own.

#### If a gripper doesn't close fully

If a gripper follows its trigger but stays partly open at full squeeze, adjust the trigger's end point before you change the motor torque:

1. Open **Mantis settings → Triggers**.
2. Lower **Left trigger full squeeze** or **Right trigger full squeeze** from its default of `1.0`. The range is `0.50` to `1.00`. If you know the trigger's reading at full squeeze, set it a little below the lowest reading. For example, for readings around `0.91` to `0.94`, set `0.90`. Otherwise, lower it in small steps.
3. Save, then stop and start **Teleoperation** again.
4. Squeeze each trigger fully and check that the gripper closes.

If the gripper closes before the trigger reaches the end of its travel, the value is too low. Raise it. If the gripper still doesn't close when the setting is right, check the gripper's jaws, linkage and motor calibration.

When both grippers close fully, record one short test take with **Collect data** before you record the rest.

This setting applies to the rig triggers, used in Mantis teleoperation and in Lighthouse and Ultimate data collection. It does not apply to Quest controller triggers.

### Mounting the cameras

Mantis can record two wrist cameras and the head stereo camera at the same time.

1. Mount up to two ZED cameras on the rigs. They mount the same way as on Axol's grippers (see [Axol Cameras](/hardware-setup/cameras)).
2. Connect each camera, including the head camera, to the computer with its GMSL2 cable.
3. Note each camera's serial number from the QR code on its back.
4. In **Mantis settings → Cameras**, assign each camera to **Head**, **Left Mantis** or **Right Mantis**.
5. Turn on **Record** for the cameras you want to record. For the head camera, turn on **Record** for both eyes to record both views.

You must turn on **Record** for at least one camera before you can collect data. The Mantis camera layout is saved separately from Axol's, so changing one doesn't change the other. In the dataset, the head camera is named `overhead`. See [Cameras](/guides/control-panel#cameras) for the stream and record controls.
