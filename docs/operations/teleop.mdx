Teleoperation drives Axol's arms live from a Meta Quest headset. You can drive the real robot or the browser **sim**. With **Mantis** selected, teleoperation drives only the two grippers from the rig triggers. Start it from the web control panel or with `axol teleop`.

## Before you start

- Install Axol with the [one-command install](/installation) on the robot's computer. For sim, a [development install](/advanced/development-install) is enough.
- Select and connect the device. In the control panel, press **Select** on the **Axol** or **Mantis** tile. The panel brings up CAN and checks the motors when it connects. On the CLI, run [`axol can.setup`](/cli/can-setup) once.
- For Axol, have a Meta Quest with the [TLS certificate authorized](#authorize-the-tls-certificate). Mantis teleoperation needs no headset.
- For Mantis, finish the [hardware setup](/mantis/hardware). Tracking is not used here, so you don't need [Mantis Tracking](/mantis/tracking) yet.

<Note>
  Sim needs no CAN setup or motor check.
</Note>

## Authorize the TLS certificate

The VR app connects to the robot over HTTPS with a self-signed certificate. Approve it once in each headset's browser, or the connection fails without an error.

1. In the VR app, press **Connect**.
2. If the certificate isn't approved yet, the connection fails and an **Authorize certificate** button appears. Tap it.
3. Proceed past the warning in the popup. The app reconnects.

To approve it by hand instead, open `https://<hostname>.local:8000` or `https://<local-ip>:8000` in the headset browser and proceed past the warning.

## Run it

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Axol">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Connect the host and robot">
        Open the control panel and connect the **Axol Host**. With **Axol** selected, the panel connects the robot and runs `can.setup` the first time. If the tile isn't green, press **Connect** on it. For sim, skip the robot connection. See [Connections](/guides/control-panel#connections).
      </Step>

      <Step title="Assign cameras (optional)">
        To see the camera feeds in the headset, open **Axol settings → Cameras** and assign the ZED cameras to the overhead, left arm and right arm slots. See [Cameras](/guides/control-panel#cameras).
      </Step>

      <Step title="Select Teleoperation and Start">
        Pick **Teleoperation** from the operation selector. Turn on **sim** to drive the browser visualizer instead of the robot. Press **Start**.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Connect the headset">
        Put on the Quest and open [axol.almond.bot](https://axol.almond.bot). Enter the host's address, press **Connect**, then press **Start**. Press both grips together to engage the arms. See the [controller layout](#quest-controller-layout).
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    **From the CLI**, on the robot's computer:

    ```bash
    axol teleop
    ```

    To drive the sim, add `--sim`. This opens a visualizer at [http://localhost:8002](http://localhost:8002) and runs the same VR server on port 8000. Use it to check the network and tracking before you power the robot.

    ```bash
    axol teleop --sim
    ```

    The terminal prints the hostname and IP address. In the headset, open [axol.almond.bot](https://axol.almond.bot), enter either address, press **Connect**, then press **Start**.

    <Tip>
      To show the ZED camera feeds in the headset, pass their serial numbers: `--cameras "{overhead: 41234567, left_arm: 41234568, right_arm: 41234569}"`. You can move and resize each feed with the controllers. See [Camera views](/guides/vr-interface#camera-views).
    </Tip>

    See [`teleop`](/cli/teleop) for every flag, including stiffness, gripper limits, per-joint gains and config files.
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Mantis">
    Mantis teleoperation drives the two grippers from the rig triggers. It uses no tracking, headset or cameras. Use it to check the hub wiring, the left and right mapping, and the grippers. To record tracked Mantis data, use [data collection](/operations/data-collection).

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Select and connect Mantis">
        Press **Select** on the **Mantis** tile. The panel connects the rigs, or you can press **Connect**. The tile shows the CAN health of both grippers. If you haven't checked the side mapping yet, do that first. See [Mantis Hardware](/mantis/hardware#selecting-and-connecting-mantis).
      </Step>

      <Step title="Start and check the grippers">
        Pick **Teleoperation** and press **Start**. Mantis has no sim or Jelly option.

        When both triggers report in and are released, the grippers open to their hard stops. Keep your hands clear. Each trigger then closes its gripper in proportion to how far you squeeze it.

        - Squeeze the **left** trigger. If the **right** gripper moves, swap the CAN side mapping.
        - If a fully squeezed trigger doesn't fully close its gripper, [check its full-squeeze endpoint](/mantis/hardware#testing-the-triggers-and-grippers).

        A faulted gripper or a trigger that stops reporting stops the run. **Stop** turns off both grippers.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    **From the CLI**, using the channel map saved in **Mantis settings**:

    ```bash
    axol teleop --mantis
    ```

    To track the rigs, finish [Mantis Tracking](/mantis/tracking), then run [data collection](/operations/data-collection).
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Quest controller layout

These bindings are for Axol. For the Mantis gestures used in data collection, see [Start gesture and recovery](/mantis/tracking#start-gesture-and-recovery).

<img src="/assets/quest.png" alt="Quest controller diagram" />

| # | Button | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Left grip | Press both grips together to **engage** both arms. After that, each grip freezes or resumes the arm on its side. |
| 2 | Right grip | Same as the left grip. |
| 3 | Left trigger | Close the left gripper. |
| 4 | Right trigger | Close the right gripper. |
| 5 | X | **Reset**: return both arms to the rest pose. Also returns the arms home from a contact hold. |
| 7 | Y | **Exit AR**: close the VR session. The arms return to rest. |

### Engaging and freezing

- From rest, press both grips together to start moving the arms.
- After that, a click on one grip freezes or resumes that arm only. A frozen arm holds its pose and its grasp, which helps steady a grasped object.
- A reset, a contact stop or a dropped link disengages both arms. Press both grips again to re-engage.

To move an arm only while you hold its grip, turn on **Hold grips to engage** (`--teleop.hold_to_engage`). Each grip then engages the arm on its side, from rest too, and releasing the grip freezes that arm. After a forced disengage, release any grip you were still holding before it engages again.

## Safety behavior

### Starting

Teleoperation, data collection, DAgger, run policy and replay refuse to start while any arm joint is more than 25° from zero. Zero is the pose the arms settle into when the motors are off. The error names each joint that is out of range. Move the arms back by hand, for example under `axol gravity-comp`, then start again.

Once the check passes, the arms move to zero and then out to the rest pose. `collect-dagger --start_from_current_pose` skips this check unless `--home_on_start` is also set.

### Returning to rest

On the real robot, every return to rest is guarded, including the **X** reset and the first move at startup. If an arm meets resistance, such as a held object or a person, the move stops.

1. The arms go limp in a **gravity-compensation hold**.
2. Guide them clear by hand.
3. Press **X** to plan a new return from where they are.

The hold has no timeout. If you leave VR or the link drops, the arms stay limp until you put the headset back on and press **X**.

Set the sensitivity with **Reset contact threshold** in **Axol settings → Robot** (`--teleop.reset_torque_threshold`). `0` turns the check off. Sim has no torque feedback, so it has no guard.

### Contact stop while driving

You can also stop the arms when they push hard while you drive them. This check is off by default, because teleoperation often pushes on the scene on purpose. Set **Teleop contact stop** in **Axol settings → Robot** (`--teleop.teleop_torque_threshold`). The panel suggests `16` Nm.

When a joint pushes past the threshold for a moment, tracking disengages and the arms go limp in the same hold. Guide them clear, then press **X** to return to rest. This works on the real robot only.

### Losing the link

If pose updates stop while the arms are engaged, the arms freeze in place after about half a second. This happens when you take off the headset, open the system menu, quit the VR app or lose the network. The arms hold that pose until you come back.

Re-entering VR doesn't move the arms. Press both grips to re-engage, and tracking resumes from where the arms are. Press **X** to send them to the rest pose.

### Stopping

When you press **Stop** in the control panel or `Ctrl+C` on the CLI, the arms return to the rest pose. With **Park to zero on stop** on (`--teleop.park_to_zero`, the default), they then move to zero and hang straight down, holding their grasp. Only then do the motors turn off, so the arms don't drop.

- Each move is guarded like a return to rest. The move to rest can take up to 6 s and the move to zero up to 10 s.
- If a move times out or hits something, the motors turn off where the arms are.
- Press `Ctrl+C` again to stop the park where it is.
- Axol skips the park in `--sim`, while the arms are limp in a contact hold, and when the IK planner or the CAN feedback has failed.

Turn off **Park to zero on stop** to stop at the rest pose.

### Driving Jelly

On Axol on Jelly, the thumbsticks drive Jelly's base and lift. See [Jelly](/guides/vr-interface#jelly). Axol detects Jelly from the CAN devices that are attached.

- To leave attached Jelly hardware out, turn off **Jelly wheels** or **Jelly lift** in the Robot tab, or pass [`--jelly.wheels false` or `--jelly.lift false`](/cli/teleop).
- To drive only Jelly, turn off **Axol arms**, or run [`axol teleop --arms false`](/cli/teleop).

## Network tip

If VR tracking feels jittery or arrives in bursts, set these on your router or access point:

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| DTIM interval | `1` |
| Beacon interval | `100` ms |
| WMM APSD (U-APSD) | disabled |

These settings stop the access point from holding packets between beacons, which causes latency spikes in VR.

## Latency tuning

Teleoperation smooths the pose stream before it reaches the arms, so tracking glitches and network jitter don't shake them. With the defaults, the arm lags your hand by about 100 to 120 ms on a clean link. Each setting below is a CLI flag or a [config file](/cli/configuration) field.

| Flag | Default | What it does | Delay it adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--vr_server.interp_smooth_window_s` | `0.12` | Smooths the pose and removes short tracking glitches. | Half the window: 60 ms at the default. The largest delay. |
| `--vr_server.interp_max_delay_s` | `0.15` | Caps the buffer that evens out bursty network delivery. | None on a clean link. Grows only while the network is jittery. |
| `--teleop.pose_cutoff` | `2.5` | Filters hand tremor out of the VR poses (Hz). | About 40 ms. |
| `--teleop.ik_alpha` | `0.3` | Smooths the joint targets. | About 19 ms at the default, 8 ms at `0.5`. |

If teleoperation feels too slow, change one setting at a time, in this order, and test each on the real robot. The same smoothing makes contact-heavy work such as deburring or insertion feel steady.

1. `--vr_server.interp_smooth_window_s 0.08` saves 20 ms and still removes most short glitches.
2. `--teleop.ik_alpha 0.4` saves a few ms with little loss of smoothness.
3. `--teleop.pose_cutoff 3.5` cuts filter delay but lets more hand tremor through. Watch for joint jitter.
4. `--vr_server.interp_smooth_window_s 0` turns the smoother off. Use it only over a [USB connection](/guides/quest-over-usb) with well-tracked controllers.

<Note>
  The network buffer only adds delay while the network is bursty. For network lag, a [USB connection](/guides/quest-over-usb) and the [router settings](#network-tip) help more than any setting here.

  If the headset's Wi-Fi stalls and then delivers a backlog, the arms don't replay the late motion. They hold, then move to your current pose when on-time frames resume. A frame counts as late when it arrives more than `--vr_server.interp_max_lag_s` behind (default `0.5` s, `0` turns this off). The log shows `VR pose stream arriving … late` when a stall starts and `VR pose stream on time again after …` when it ends.
</Note>
