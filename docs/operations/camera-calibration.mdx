---
title: "Calibrating the Cameras"
description: "Measure where the cameras sit on your Axol so MCAP episodes place them from measurements, not CAD."
---

MCAP episodes place each camera in the transform tree. Without a camera calibration, those positions come from CAD. `axol calibration.cameras` measures where the head camera and each wrist camera sit on your Axol and saves the result. Episodes use it from the next episode on.

Calibrate after you fit the Camera Kit. Calibrate again after you move, remount or replace a camera, or after an arm is re-zeroed.

## What you need

- Axol with the Camera Kit, with the head and wrist cameras assigned in the control panel's camera layout.
- A printer, and something rigid and flat to mount the board on, such as foam board.
- A clear table in front of Axol, in view of the head camera.
- About 15 minutes.

## Print the board

```bash
axol calibration.cameras --print_board ~/calibration_board.pdf
```

1. Print the page at 100% (actual size). For A4 paper, add `--board.paper a4`.
2. Tape it flat to the rigid board. Bumps and curls make the result worse.
3. Measure one square with a ruler. It should be 40 mm. If it isn't, give the size you measured when you calibrate, for example `--board.square_mm 39.5`.

## Calibrate

The arms go limp in gravity compensation while the tool runs, so you can move them by hand.

1. Run `axol calibration.cameras` on the robot.
2. Lay the board flat on the table where the head camera sees it. Press Enter.
3. Move the left arm by hand until its wrist camera points at the board, 20 to 40 cm away, then let go. When the arm is still, the tool takes a view and prints it.
4. Take about four views from different angles. Between views, tilt the camera toward the board from another side, and turn it about its own axis.
5. Do the same with the right arm.
6. Press Enter, move the board to another spot on the table, and press Enter again. Views pause while you move it.
7. Repeat steps 3 to 6 until the board has been in at least three places, spread across the table. Four places is better.
8. Type `done` and press Enter. The tool solves and prints a result for each camera.
9. Rest both arms on something, then press Enter to stop. The arms go limp when the tool stops.

Keep your hands and body out of the head camera's view of the board when you can. Each wrist camera needs at least 10 views in total.

## Read the result

Each camera gets `PASS` or `FAIL`, with what the tool measured:

```
Head camera: PASS. 24 views, error 0.62 px, 3.8 mm and 0.41 degrees from CAD.
Left wrist camera: PASS. 14 views, error 1.10 px, 2.1 mm and 0.93 degrees from CAD, wrist turned 34 and 15 degrees.
```

A camera passes when all of these hold:

| Check | Passes when |
|---|---|
| Views | A wrist camera has at least 10 views. The head camera saw the board in at least 3 places. |
| Error | The board corners land within 3 px, on average, of where the calibration puts them. |
| Wrist turn | The wrist turned about at least two different axes between views. |
| Distance from CAD | The camera is within 25 mm and 6 degrees of its CAD position. |

When every camera passes, the tool saves the calibration to `~/.almond/camera_calibration.json`.

When a camera fails, nothing is saved, and the previous calibration stays in use. Fix the problem and run the tool again:

| Message | What to do |
|---|---|
| `capture at least 10` | Take more views with that arm. |
| `move it to at least 3 places` | Move the board to more spots, each one seen by the head camera and a wrist camera. |
| `turned too little` | Tilt the wrist camera toward the board from more sides, not only from above. |
| `from CAD` | Check the camera mount is tight, then calibrate again. |
| `reprojection error` | Check the board is flat and the square size is right. Hold the arm still for each view. |

Every run keeps a report folder, `~/.almond/camera_calibration/run-<time>/`. Its `checked` folder has each view with green circles where the tool found the board corners and red crosses where the calibration puts them. On a good calibration they overlap.

## How episodes use the calibration

At the start of every episode, Collect data reads `~/.almond/camera_calibration.json`. It uses the file only when it passed and names this robot. The robot is named by its Axol hub serial.

- Each measured camera is placed from the file. A lookup between the two frames in the file gives the file's values.
- A camera that was swapped after calibrating keeps its CAD position until you calibrate again.
- Without a usable file, episodes use the CAD positions. When you start Collect data, it says why the file isn't used.

The episode's `calibration` metadata says which calibration was used and where each transform came from. See [Camera mounts](/operations/mcap#camera-mounts).

## Solve again from a report

To solve a saved run again without the robot, for example with different limits:

```bash
axol calibration.cameras --from_report ~/.almond/camera_calibration/run-20261124T181500Z
```

It prints the result and writes it to a `resolved` folder inside the report. Add `--save_from_report true` to save the calibration file when every camera passes.

## Use your own calibration

You can measure the cameras another way, for example with motion capture, and write the file yourself. Collect data reads any file in this form:

```json
{
  "robot_serial": "E66138528F2B3F2A",
  "calibration_id": "lab-2026-11-24",
  "calibrated_at": "2026-11-24T18:15:00Z",
  "status": "pass",
  "transforms": [
    {"parent": "root", "child": "head_camera_left_optical",
     "translation_m": [0.07, 0.03, 0.41], "rotation_xyzw": [-0.61, 0.61, -0.36, 0.36]}
  ]
}
```

- `robot_serial` is the robot's Axol hub serial, the `robot.serial` value in any MCAP episode it recorded.
- Each transform gives the child frame's pose in the parent frame: the translation in metres and a unit quaternion in `x, y, z, w` order.
- The parent must be fixed to the child, with no moving joint between them.
- `camera_serial` is optional. When it is set, the transform applies only while that camera is fitted.
- List only the frames you measured. Every other frame comes from the URDF.

| Camera | Child frame | Parent frame |
|---|---|---|
| Head camera, left eye | `head_camera_left_optical` | `root` |
| Left wrist camera | `left_wrist_camera_optical` | `left_w2` |
| Right wrist camera | `right_wrist_camera_optical` | `right_w2` |

Optical frames have z forward out of the lens, x to the right and y down in the image. The right head eye follows the left eye by the head camera's factory stereo calibration, unless the file also lists `head_camera_right_optical`.
