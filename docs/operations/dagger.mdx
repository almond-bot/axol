---
title: "DAgger Collection"
description: "Run a trained policy, correct it from VR, and record the corrections to retrain it."
---

DAgger collection runs a trained policy on Axol while you watch in VR. When the policy goes wrong, you take over with the controllers and correct it. Axol records both the policy's motion and your corrections into a [LeRobot](/api/lerobot) dataset.

Retraining on that data teaches the policy the cases where it struggles. The method is called [DAgger](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686).

The policy drives the arms as in [Run Policy](/operations/run-policy), and you take over as in [Data Collection](/operations/data-collection). Run it on the same computer: the one wired to the robot with the ZED cameras attached. Start it from the web control panel or with `axol collect-dagger`.

## Before you start

- Install Axol with the [one-command install](/installation). Bring up CAN and check the motors.
- Connect at least one of the ZED cameras the policy was trained on, and have their serial numbers ready. See the [Data Collection](/operations/data-collection#before-you-start) tip for how to list them. Use the **same** cameras, resolution, stereo setting and recording rate the policy was trained on.
- Have a trained checkpoint and its policy type (`act`, `smolvla`, `pi0` and so on). The checkpoint can be a local path or a HuggingFace repo.
- Inference runs on this computer. You need a PyTorch build with CUDA, or pass `--device cpu`. On a Jetson, the hosted install comes with the CPU-only PyTorch build. Axol's updater doesn't replace a JetPack-compatible build you installed yourself.
- [Authorize the VR certificate](/operations/teleop#authorize-the-tls-certificate) and have the headset ready. You correct the policy from it.

<Warning>
  Load only checkpoints and datasets that you made or got from a source you trust. Model and dataset loading is not sandboxed.
</Warning>

## Run it

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Control Panel">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Connect the robot and assign cameras">
        Connect the **Axol Host** and **Axol**. Under **Axol settings → Cameras**, assign at least one of the cameras the policy was trained on, with recording on. See [Cameras](/guides/control-panel#cameras).
      </Step>

      <Step title="Select Collect DAgger data and fill in the fields">
        Pick **Collect DAgger data** and fill in **Policy path**, **Policy type**, **Task** and **Repo ID**. To add to an existing DAgger dataset, enter its repo ID. Stiffness, rates and inference settings are in the [Settings](/guides/control-panel#settings) tabs.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Start, then correct from the headset">
        Press **Start** and connect the headset. Start and end episodes from the **Episode control** box. Use the **grips** to take over and hand back during an episode, as described in [Intervening](#intervening). The panel shows the camera feeds and episode state, so you can follow along with the headset off.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="CLI">
    On the robot's computer:

    ```bash
    axol collect-dagger \
        --policy_path myorg/pick-place-policy \
        --policy_type act \
        --task "Pick the red cube and place it in the bin" \
        --repo_id myorg/pick-place-dagger \
        --robot_config.cameras "{overhead: {serial: 41234567}, left_arm: {serial: 41234568}, right_arm: {serial: 41234569}}"
    ```

    For CPU inference, add `--device cpu`. Replace the serial numbers with your cameras' and assign the cameras the policy was trained on. The `cameras` value is one inline YAML dictionary. See [Command configuration](/cli/configuration#field-name-conventions).

    Connect the headset and correct the policy from the controllers. In the terminal, `s` saves, `r` discards and `q` quits. Press `Ctrl+C` to finish. See [`collect-dagger`](/cli/collect-dagger) for every field.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Intervening

During an episode, the policy drives the arms. Use the VR grip buttons to take over when it needs help, and to hand back when it has recovered:

| When | Press | Result | Recording |
|---|---|---|---|
| The **policy** is driving | **Either** grip | **Freeze**: the robot holds its pose. | Pauses |
| The arms are **frozen** | **Both** grips | **Take over**: you drive from where the arms are. | Resumes |
| **You** are driving | **Either** grip | **Hand back**: the policy drives again. | Continues |

- Tracking starts from where the arms are, so the arms don't jump when you take over.
- The grippers follow your triggers as soon as you take over. Set the triggers before you press the grips. To keep a part held, hold the trigger down.
- When you hand back, the policy plans from where you left the arms.
- Nothing is recorded while the arms are frozen. The episode runs straight from the policy's motion into your correction and back.

With a custom policy, the grips work differently. Hold a grip to drive that arm, and release both to hand back. See [Run Your Own Policy](/operations/custom-policy#collect-corrections-with-dagger).

<Note>
  If the headset link drops while you drive, tracking disengages and the policy takes over again.
</Note>

## Episodes

You start and end episodes from VR, as in [data collection](/operations/data-collection#recording-an-episode):

- Press **Record** (**A**) to start an episode. The policy starts driving and recording begins.
- Press **Record** again to save the episode.
- Hold **Reset** (**X**) and press **Record** to discard the episode and record it again.
- In the terminal, `s` saves, `r` discards and `q` discards and quits.
- `--episode_time_s` limits each episode, 600 s by default. When it runs out, the episode is saved.
- With `--subtasks` set, type a number from 1 to N to switch the policy's instruction during the episode. The dataset keeps `--task` as its task.

The panel's **Episode control** box has the same **Start episode**, **Save**, **Discard** and **Return to rest** buttons as [run policy](/guides/control-panel#episode-controls), so you can run a session with the headset off.

### Between episodes

Between episodes, the arms return to the rest pose, so every episode starts in the same place.

- The grips work as plain teleoperation, so you can reset the scene with the arms. Press both grips to engage and one to release. Nothing is recorded.
- Press **Reset** in VR to send the arms home. If you leave the arms away from rest, they also return home when you press **Record**.

Every return to rest is guarded, as in [data collection](/operations/data-collection) and [run policy](/operations/run-policy). If an arm meets resistance, the move stops and the arms go limp in a **gravity-compensation hold**. Move them clear by hand, then press **Reset** in VR or **Return to rest** in the panel. Set the sensitivity with **Return-to-rest contact stop** in the panel, or `--teleop_config.vr_teleop_config.reset_torque_threshold` (default `6.0` Nm, `0` turns it off).

## What gets recorded

Each saved episode is one continuous take: the policy's motion, your correction, then the policy again, with the frozen gaps left out. Each frame has an **`intervention`** flag. It is `True` on frames you drove and `False` on the policy's frames, so training can weight or filter your corrections.

DAgger datasets save and resume like [`collect-data`](/cli/collect-data) datasets. You can resume only a dataset that has the `intervention` flag, which means one recorded with `collect-dagger`.
