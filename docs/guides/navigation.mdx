---
title: "Mapping and Navigation"
description: "Map a space with the lidar, save waypoints, and drive Axol on Jelly to them."
---

With a [lidar](/hardware-setup/lidar) on the robot, Axol on Jelly can find its way around a space you have mapped. You map the space once, save the places it should go as waypoints, and then send it to them.

<Warning>
  Jelly drives itself in this mode. Keep the E-Stop within reach and people out of its way. Put the arms at rest before you start: navigation doesn't move them, and it only keeps the base clear of obstacles.
</Warning>

## Before you start

- Set up the lidar with [`lidar.setup`](/cli/lidar-setup), including `--measure-mount`.
- Run navigation on the Compute Kit that Jelly is connected to.
- Stop teleoperation first. Only one program can drive Jelly at a time.

## Make a map

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick a starting spot">
    Put the robot where the map should start, such as a charging spot or a mark on the floor. This spot becomes the map's origin. Starting each later session here lets the robot find itself right away.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start mapping">
    ```bash
    axol nav.map office
    ```

    Open `http://<robot>:8010` to watch the map grow.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Drive through the space">
    Drive the robot with the headset, using teleoperation from the control panel. The teleop screen shows the live lidar scan, and the [Sensors](/guides/control-panel#sensors) page shows the map so far. Drive slowly, turn slowly, and pass through every area and doorway the robot should reach. Come back to the starting spot at the end.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save it">
    Press **Save map** in the map view, or Ctrl-C in the terminal.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Map an empty space if you can. A cell counts as an obstacle when the lidar saw something there at a height the robot could hit. People who walked through while you mapped are mostly removed, but objects that stood still for a while stay in the map. Map again after you move furniture.

The map is saved in `~/.almond/maps/office/`. `map.png` is a picture of it: white is clear floor, black is an obstacle, and grey is unexplored.

## Drive to waypoints

```bash
axol nav.run office
```

`nav.run` finds the robot on the map and opens the map view at `http://<robot>:8010`. The view shows the map, the robot, the live scan in orange and the planned path in green.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Go to waypoint** | Drive to the waypoint chosen in **Waypoint**. |
| **Visit all in order** | Drive to every waypoint in turn. Tick **Repeat** to keep going. |
| **Click a goal on the map** | Drive to the point you click next. |
| **Stop** | Stop right away. |
| **Save current position** | Save where the robot is now as a waypoint, under **Name**. |
| **Delete selected waypoint** | Delete the waypoint chosen in **Waypoint**. |
| **Set robot pose by clicking** | Tell the robot where it is: click where it stands, then a point it faces. |

To save waypoints, drive the robot to each place with the headset, then press **Save current position**. You can also add waypoints by their coordinates. See [`nav.waypoint`](/cli/nav-waypoint).

To drive from a script, use [`nav.goto`](/cli/nav-run). It exits when the robot arrives:

```bash
axol nav.goto office bench
axol nav.goto office bench dock --loop
```

## How it drives

- The robot plans the shortest route that keeps it 0.45 m from obstacles, and keeps to the middle of doorways and corridors.
- It drives at up to 0.3 m/s. Change this with `--speed`, up to 0.7 m/s.
- It stops for anything in its way that isn't on the map, such as a person or a box. After 3 seconds it looks for a way around. After 60 seconds it gives up.
- It slows to half speed when something is within 0.7 m of the base.
- It stops if it can no longer match its scan to the map. The status shows **Not localized**.

To see where the robot is on a saved map without driving it, pick the map on the [Sensors](/guides/control-panel#sensors) page.

## When the robot can't find itself

When `nav.run` starts, the robot looks for itself near where it was last, or near the map's origin. If it can't find a clear match, the status shows **Not localized** and it won't drive.

- Drive the robot back to where mapping started, or
- press **Set robot pose by clicking** and click where the robot is, then a point it faces, or
- start with `--start X Y HEADING`, in metres and degrees on the map.

## Limits

- The lidar can't see low objects close to the robot, glass, or thin rails. Keep these out of its path.
- The map is flat: ramps and stairs aren't supported.
- Navigation keeps the base clear, not the arms. Keep the arms at rest.

## Try it in simulation

You can try mapping and navigation without hardware, in a simulated office:

```bash
axol nav.map demo --sim
axol nav.waypoint demo add bench 6.5 -0.5 90
axol nav.run demo --sim
```
